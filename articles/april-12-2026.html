This week's edition covers six developments reshaping credit access, group lending, open finance regulation, and neobank growth across Latin America — all directly relevant to Prosperas and its mission to bring financial inclusion to women entrepreneurs through group lending in Mexico.
🏦 Neobanks · Mexico

Plata Eyes $4.5–5B Valuation as Mexico's Credit-First Neobank Prepares LatAm Expansion

Mexico's digital bank Plata, which launched with a credit-first model targeting underserved consumers, is in discussions to raise a new round targeting a valuation north of $4.5–5 billion — up from $3.3 billion earlier this year and more than triple its 2025 Series A. The company secured its full banking license from the CNBV in December 2024 and is now positioned to expand beyond Mexico, with Colombia as its first international market and other high-growth economies under evaluation. Plata's thesis mirrors Nubank's Brazilian playbook: start with credit products for the underbanked, then layer in a full suite of banking services.

📌 Prosperas Relevance: Validates the credit-first model for underserved populations in Mexico. Plata's expansion into group-adjacent markets signals growing investor confidence in serving the unbanked and underbanked through digital credit rails.
🌎 Financial Inclusion · Mexico

Nu Mexico Surpasses 13 Million Customers, Reaches 98% of Municipalities

Nu Mexico — the local arm of Nubank — has crossed 13 million customers and now operates in 98% of Mexico's municipalities, including rural communities designated as government priority zones. More than 60% of Nu's Mexican customers belong to low or lower-middle-income households. The company's banking license (the first SOFIPO-to-bank conversion approved by Mexico's CNBV) will enable it to introduce payroll accounts, a critical product in a country where only 36% of adults have this basic instrument. Nu's 100% digital model has proven particularly effective at reaching first-time formal banking users in smaller cities and rural areas.

📌 Prosperas Relevance: Demonstrates the scale of Mexico's underserved market and the success of mobile-first approaches. Prosperas' target segment — women entrepreneurs in non-metro areas — overlaps directly with Nu's growth regions.
⚖️ Open Finance · Chile

Chile Activates Mandatory Open Finance in April 2026 — A Regional Milestone

Chile's Financial Market Commission (CMF) has begun the mandatory phase of its Open Finance System this month, making Chile the first country in Latin America to fully activate compulsory open finance data sharing. Financial institutions must now make account, transaction, and credit history data available via standardized APIs to third-party providers. The move is expected to lower barriers for alternative lenders and credit scoring platforms, as open data enables more accurate risk assessment for thin-file borrowers — a critical gap for women entrepreneurs and informal-economy participants across the region.

📌 Prosperas Relevance: Chile's open finance activation will generate learnings applicable across LatAm. Mexico's own open finance regulation (Ley Fintech) continues to evolve, and tools built on open data will increasingly power alternative credit models like group lending.
💳 BNPL · LatAm

LatAm BNPL Market Expands to $14.9B, Eyes $43B by 2031 as Omnichannel Takes Hold

A new market report confirms that Latin America's Buy Now Pay Later sector reached $14.9 billion in 2025, after growing at a 32.8% CAGR during 2022–2025. The sector is projected to expand to $43 billion by 2031 as BNPL moves beyond e-commerce into in-store and omnichannel financing. Brazil and Colombia are leading regulatory activity in the space: Brazil continues to refine fintech lender supervision, while Colombia has expanded its digital credit regulatory sandbox. The shift toward in-store BNPL is particularly significant for micro and small businesses, which often serve as both users and potential distribution points for these products.

📌 Prosperas Relevance: BNPL growth signals strong consumer credit demand across income segments. For group lending players, BNPL's expansion into informal retail creates both competitive dynamics and potential partnership opportunities with micro-merchant networks.
👩‍💼 Women & Credit · Mexico

Women's Credit Access in Mexico Stalls at 18.3% — Fintechs Step In

Despite Mexico hosting 795 active fintechs at end of 2025, formal credit access for women remains at just 18.3% of the adult female population, far below the national average of 37.3%. The Finnovista Fintech Radar Mexico 2026 found that 34.5% of fintech companies operate with products specifically designed for underserved groups. Platforms like Tappoyo (group lending across Mexico, Peru, Colombia, Ecuador), Microwd (investor-to-woman-entrepreneur direct financing), and Konfío (AI-powered SME credit) are among the most active in addressing this gap. Companies with more than 50% women in leadership positions are twice as likely to build inclusive credit products.

📌 Prosperas Relevance: This is Prosperas' core market. The data confirms both the scale of the opportunity (women's credit gap) and the momentum of tech-enabled group lending as a proven mechanism for serving this segment at scale.
📱 Payments · Peru

Peru's Payments Ecosystem Surges to 120 Paytechs as Yape Tops 17 Million Users

Peru's payments landscape has grown rapidly, with 120 regulated paytechs now active in the country. Yape, the super-app developed by Banco de Crédito del Perú (BCP), has surpassed 17 million users across Peru and Bolivia. Originally a P2P transfer app, Yape now offers microcredits, international remittances, bill payments, and e-commerce — all commission-free and QR-code powered. Open finance regulation in Peru is expected to accelerate further consolidation, with smaller paytechs likely to partner with established platforms like Yape rather than compete directly.

📌 Prosperas Relevance: Yape's microcredit layer and super-app expansion mirrors the product evolution many group lending platforms are pursuing. The Peru case provides a roadmap for how digital wallets can become entry points for structured credit products for underserved users.
📊 Regulation · Colombia

Colombia Emerges as LatAm Open Finance Leader with Comprehensive Draft Decree

Colombia published a comprehensive mandatory Open Finance draft decree in June 2025, and implementation preparations are now entering a critical phase in Q2 2026. The framework mandates that banks, insurers, and financial intermediaries expose customer data via APIs to authorized third parties. Separately, Colombia's digital credit regulatory sandbox has been expanded to accommodate more BNPL and alternative lending business models. With Plata also identifying Colombia as its first international expansion target, the country is positioned to become a key battleground for LatAm's credit-for-the-unbanked movement.

📌 Prosperas Relevance: Colombia's open finance and regulatory sandbox activity accelerates the infrastructure for alternative lenders. As group lending models seek to expand regionally, Colombia's framework offers a structured path to market entry for players like Prosperas.
