Credit in LatAm
What It Means for Prosperas

Current state, key trends, who is winning and why — and what Prosperas can take from it.

Context

$50BLatAm fintech market by 2033

The LatAm fintech market reached $13.1B in 2024 and is projected to grow to $49.6B by 2033 (15.9% CAGR). Credit is the second-largest fintech segment after payments, representing ~19% of total activity. This is no longer a speculative bet — capital is flowing at scale because the data confirms it works.

The SME credit gap was valued at $509B in 2023 and is projected to reach $923B by 2026. In Mexico alone, credit fintechs granted over $3B in loans in 2023, benefiting more than 5 million users. The gap is real, large, and being captured by those who move fastest.

Direct relevance for Prosperas: The segment it serves — women entrepreneurs without formal credit history — is exactly where the gap is largest and where the historical performance of microfinance has shown that actual risk is far lower than perceived risk.

Trends
The 4 forces redefining credit in the region
AI-powered underwriting, embedded credit, women as the fastest-growing segment, and regulation maturing into a competitive moat.
1. AI is rewriting underwriting

Credit models that previously took 9–12 months to build are now deployed in 3. Leading companies use behavioral data, transaction patterns, and geolocation instead of — or in addition to — bureau scores. Nubank holds a 4.4% NPL rate vs. the 5.5% Brazilian banking sector average, while serving customers that were previously considered unbankable. AI isn't just cutting costs — it's enabling better credit decisions on populations with no formal history.

2. Embedded credit is winning on distribution

R2, one of the fastest-growing lenders in Mexico and Colombia, doesn't acquire customers directly. It embeds credit into platforms like UberEats, Rappi, and PayU — where the merchant's cash flow is already observable. The embedded finance market in Mexico alone is projected to exceed $22B by 2030. Credit should flow to where the customer already is — not wait for the customer to come to credit.

3. Women are the fastest-growing credit segment in Mexico

Fintechs reduced the gender gap in credit access from 8.8% to 2.6% between 2023 and 2025. Women now represent 43% of first-time credit recipients and hold 51% of active credits. This is not an impact narrative — it's a high-performing segment that traditional banks systematically underserved and that fintechs are now capturing at scale.

4. Regulation is maturing into a competitive moat

Brazil leads with Open Finance; Mexico, Colombia, and Peru are following. Companies that build regulatory capacity early turn it into a barrier to entry. Investors in 2026 are prioritizing "intelligent growth" — profitability, operational efficiency, and regulatory readiness — over raw user acquisition. Fintech now accounts for 61% of total VC investment in the region.

Application
What Prosperas can take from all of this
Five concrete levers directly derived from what is working in the market today — each maps onto Prosperas' existing strengths.
1. First-party data is the most valuable asset — build the flywheel deliberately

Prosperas' model of consumer-consented, non-traditional first-party data is exactly the asset Nubank has built at 123M-customer scale. Every 16-week cycle, every repayment, every group dynamic is a data point that can sharpen the next underwriting decision. The priority is to build this feedback loop explicitly — not leave it as a byproduct. This is what separates lenders that compound in quality from those that plateau.

2. Expand distribution through partnerships, not just direct acquisition

R2 proved you don't need to be the first touchpoint. Prosperas can explore embedding credit access through platforms that already serve women entrepreneurs — marketplaces, supply chains, cooperative networks, digital commerce tools. The customer should encounter credit where she already transacts, not have to seek it out separately.

3. The women's segment has stopped being a niche — capitalize now

The reduction of the gender credit gap from 8.8% to 2.6% in Mexico in three years means the market is being repriced around a segment Prosperas has served since before it was fashionable. That's a durable advantage — data, brand trust, and methodology — but only if it's leveraged before better-funded players arrive. The window to establish defensible leadership is now.

4. Evolve the 16-week cycle toward progressive credit limits

The installment discipline that makes BNPL sticky is built into Prosperas' group lending model — plus social accountability that BNPL doesn't have. The question worth testing: can cycles shorten, personalize, or tier for customers with proven history? Nubank systematically raises credit limits as behavioral data accumulates. The same logic applied to group lending could unlock significantly higher LTV per borrower without adding underwriting risk.

5. Build infrastructure for Open Finance before it's mandatory

The convergence of Open Finance frameworks across Mexico, Colombia, and the broader region will eventually allow credit histories to port across institutions and enable richer consumer data usage. Building the infrastructure to connect to these frameworks early creates a moat that informal lenders and late-moving incumbents cannot quickly replicate.

The market is validating everything Prosperas does. The next phase is acceleration: faster model iteration, broader distribution via partnerships, and treating every credit cycle as a compounding data asset — not just a transaction.
