LatAm Credit Pulse: Nubank Goes Full Bank in Mexico, Konfío's $2.5B Push & Open Finance Milestones

Six stories shaping credit access, women's financial inclusion, and fintech regulation across Latin America this week.

Week of March 16–22, 2026
Neobanks 🇲🇽 Mexico

Nubank Activates Full Banking License in Mexico, Targeting 90 Million Unbanked Adults

Nubank's Mexican arm has officially activated its full banking license — approved by the CNBV — marking one of the most consequential milestones in LatAm fintech this year. The Brazilian neobank already serves 13 million customers in Mexico, representing approximately 14% of all adults and 25% of the country's banked population.

With a full banking license, Nubank can now offer deposits, broader credit products, and build deeper customer relationships — a major step toward competing directly with Mexico's legacy banks.

Mexico remains one of the largest financial inclusion opportunities in Latin America, with an estimated 17 million adult women still completely excluded from formal financial services. Nubank's scale and digital-first approach positions it to reach underserved segments that traditional banks have long ignored.

Why it matters for Prosperas

As Nubank deepens its credit infrastructure in Mexico, it validates the massive demand for accessible, tech-driven financial products targeting unbanked and underbanked women — the same population Prosperas serves through group lending.

SME Credit 🇲🇽 Mexico

Konfío Commits $2.5 Billion to Mexican SME Financing Through 2028

Mexican digital lending fintech Konfío has announced plans to deploy US$2.5 billion in SME financing between 2026 and 2028, targeting approximately 85,000 businesses currently without access to formal credit. In parallel, the company will invest 7 billion pesos (approx. $406M) to strengthen its technology infrastructure and compliance systems.

Konfío's commitment is contingent on receiving its own full banking license — which, if approved, would allow it to offer deposits and expand its lending book significantly.

Mexico's SME credit gap remains enormous. At the end of 2025, 795 active fintechs operated in the country — more than half focused on payments and credit — yet one in three SMEs still reports facing credit restrictions. Women-led microenterprises face even steeper barriers, including gender bias in lending decisions and lack of traditional collateral.

Why it matters for Prosperas

Konfío's scale push signals strong investor confidence in Mexico's SME credit market. For Prosperas, this underscores the commercial viability of lending to underserved entrepreneurs — and the urgency of capturing market share before the space becomes more competitive.

Group Lending 🌎 LatAm

Group Lending Models for Women Gain Tech Momentum Across the Region

Two fintech companies are scaling community-based credit models designed specifically for women entrepreneurs outside the formal financial system. Tappoyo uses group lending with digital workflows to extend fair and fast loans across Mexico, Colombia, Peru, and Ecuador — harnessing solidarity dynamics to reduce default risk. Grupago, founded in 2023, combines AI underwriting, group collateral, and community distribution to reach women-led small businesses that lack credit history.

Research continues to validate the model's effectiveness: the J-PAL evaluation of microcredit programs in Mexico found that access to group loans led to measurable increases in business investment and household consumption among women borrowers — particularly those running informal microenterprises.

73% of women entrepreneurs in Latin America cannot access support from formal financial institutions, yet women-led groups consistently demonstrate superior repayment rates in solidarity lending models.
Why it matters for Prosperas

The growing ecosystem of tech-enabled group lenders validates Prosperas' core model. The emergence of AI-augmented underwriting within group lending specifically presents opportunities to improve efficiency and scale faster while maintaining the community trust that drives repayment.

BNPL 🌎 LatAm

LatAm BNPL Market Surges 24.8% to $18.5 Billion in 2026, Extending Beyond E-Commerce

Latin America's Buy Now, Pay Later market is projected to reach $18.5 billion in 2026, up 24.8% year-over-year, according to a new market report. The sector is forecast to grow at a compound annual growth rate of 18.3% through 2031, ultimately exceeding $43 billion.

Critically, BNPL is expanding well beyond e-commerce into in-store and omnichannel financing, with super apps and local e-commerce platforms driving much of the growth. Kueski — one of Mexico's largest BNPL and consumer lending platforms — processes nearly one million app downloads per month and has issued approximately 40 million loans to date. In February 2026, the company hired a former Robinhood, Nubank, and Capital One executive as its VP of Fraud, signaling a push for greater operational sophistication.

The BNPL boom is creating new consumer credit behavior among millions of previously unbanked Mexicans and Colombians — including first-time borrowers who are building credit profiles for the first time.
Why it matters for Prosperas

As BNPL normalizes installment-based credit among informal entrepreneurs, the audience for Prosperas' group lending products grows more financially literate and credit-aware — reducing onboarding friction and improving repayment culture over time.

Open Finance 🇨🇱 Chile · 🇲🇽 Mexico

Chile's Open Finance Law Enters Mandatory Phase in April; Mexico Still Awaiting Framework

Chile is set to begin mandatory implementation of its Open Finance Law in April 2026 — making it one of the first LatAm countries to formalize data sharing across financial institutions. The law requires banks, insurers, and fintechs to offer standardized APIs enabling customers to port their financial data to third-party providers, unlocking new credit scoring and personalized product possibilities.

Mexico, by contrast, remains in a holding pattern. According to the Finnovista Radar Mexico 2026, open finance in the country is "back to the discussion" but the regulatory framework remains "under construction" — with a notable decline in the number of active open finance players due to regulatory uncertainty. Mexico's broader Fintech Law 2.0 update was expected but has yet to be finalized.

Chile's implementation will serve as a critical regional test case. If it succeeds in expanding credit access for underserved populations, it will strengthen the policy case for Mexico and Colombia to accelerate their own frameworks.
Why it matters for Prosperas

Open finance infrastructure — when it arrives in Mexico — will allow lenders like Prosperas to access richer borrower data, reduce reliance on traditional credit bureaus, and more accurately score the women entrepreneurs who currently lack formal credit histories.

Financial Inclusion 🇲🇽 Mexico

Mexico's Fintech Ecosystem Shifts Toward Underserved Segments, With Women-Led Leadership as Key Driver

A landmark finding from the Finnovista Fintech Radar Mexico 2026 reveals that 34.5% of Mexican fintech companies now operate with products specifically designed for underserved groups — a notable shift from the broader market focus of prior years. Mexico's 795 active fintechs are increasingly developing targeted solutions for women, rural populations, and informal workers.

Perhaps the most striking insight: companies where more than 50% of leadership positions are held by women are twice as likely to develop genuinely inclusive financial products. The report calls this the "Leadership Effect" — establishing diverse talent as the primary structural driver of financial democratization in Mexico.

Despite progress, the inclusion gap remains stark. Seventeen million adult Mexican women are completely excluded from the financial system, while 12 million more have access to only a single financial product.

The fastest-growing fintech segment in Mexico is not payments or crypto — it is credit solutions for people with no formal financial history.
Why it matters for Prosperas

The "Leadership Effect" finding directly reinforces Prosperas' organizational DNA. As a company built around serving women entrepreneurs, Prosperas is structurally positioned to lead the kind of genuine inclusion that the broader ecosystem is now beginning to prioritize.

Impact Investment 🌎 Emerging Markets

IFC and Fasanara Partner to Scale MSME Financing Across Emerging Markets

The International Finance Corporation (IFC) and London-based alternative asset manager Fasanara Capital have launched a new strategy to significantly expand access to finance for micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) in emerging markets — including Latin America. The partnership will leverage Fasanara's embedded lending and supply chain finance technology alongside IFC's institutional capital and market development expertise.

The initiative targets the persistent "missing middle" — businesses that are too large for microfinance programs but too small and informal to access traditional bank lending. Women-led businesses are disproportionately represented in this gap, particularly in Mexico, where informality remains high and collateral requirements frequently exclude female entrepreneurs.

IFC estimates the MSME financing gap in Latin America exceeds $300 billion — with women-owned businesses accounting for a disproportionate share of unmet demand.
Why it matters for Prosperas

The IFC-Fasanara partnership signals that institutional capital is increasingly flowing toward tech-enabled, impact-focused MSME lenders in emerging markets. For Prosperas, this represents both a potential funding avenue and validation that the group lending model for women entrepreneurs is attracting serious development finance attention.
